# How to post a story on the Ongera Ubeho website

**No coding. No website knowledge needed.** If you can use Facebook or Word, you can do this.

Every time Ongera Ubeho does something — a community session, street outreach, a
training, a partnership — you can put it on the website yourself, with photos, in about
10 minutes.

---

## First time only: get access (5 minutes)

You need a free GitHub account. GitHub is where the website's files live — you will
never have to understand it, you just need an account so the system knows it is you.

1. Go to **github.com** and click **Sign up**. Use the Ongera Ubeho email.
2. Tell Godwin your GitHub username so he can give you access to the website.
3. Go to **app.pagescms.org** and click **Sign in with GitHub**.
4. Allow access when GitHub asks.
5. Choose the repository called **OngeraUbeho**.

That is it. From now on you only ever go to **app.pagescms.org**.

> **Tip:** Bookmark app.pagescms.org in your browser so you can find it quickly.

---

## Posting a story (every time)

### 1. Open the editor
Go to **app.pagescms.org**, sign in, and open **OngeraUbeho**.
In the left menu click **News & Field Reports**.

You will see all the stories already published. Click the green **Add an entry**
button (top right) to start a new one.

### 2. Fill in the boxes

| Box | What to write |
|---|---|
| **Title** | The headline. Short and human. *"Mentor training begins in Gasabo"* — not *"Training Report"*. |
| **Date of the action** | The day it actually happened. Click the calendar and pick it. |
| **Report number** | `Field Report 02`, then `03` next time, and so on. |
| **Place** | e.g. `Kigali, Rwanda` or `Gasabo District` |
| **Short summary** | 2–3 sentences. **This is important** — it is what people see on Facebook and WhatsApp when the link is shared. |
| **Main photo** | The big photo at the top. Click, then upload from your computer or phone. Pick the one that best shows the work happening. |
| **Main photo caption** | One line describing that photo. |
| **The story** | Write what happened (see the writing tips below). |
| **Highlight sentence** | Optional. One powerful sentence shown large in a box. |
| **More photos** | Click **Add** for each extra photo, and give each one a caption. |
| **Show the privacy note** | **Leave this ON** whenever there are photos of young people or families. |

### 3. Publish
Click **Save** at the top right.

**Your story is now live.** Wait about one minute, then open
**ongeraubeho.rw/news** and refresh — it will be there, with its own link you can share
on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and LinkedIn.

## Writing tips (this is what makes donors care)

**Use section headings.** In the story box, highlight a line and choose **Heading**.
Good headings to reuse every time:

- *What we did*
- *What we saw / What we heard*
- *What happens next*

**Write like you are telling a friend.** Short sentences. Real details.

> ❌ "A sensitization activity was conducted with beneficiaries."
> ✅ "We sat down with twelve families in one room. For most of them it was the first
> time anyone had asked what was happening at home."

**Use real numbers whenever you have them.** "We spoke with 23 young people and 14
parents" is worth more to a funder than "many people attended".

**Be honest about what did not work.** Funders trust organisations that say
"this part was harder than we expected" far more than ones that only report success.

## Photo rules (please follow these)

1. **Ask permission before you photograph anyone** — especially young people. If a
   parent or guardian says no, do not use that photo.
2. **Never name a young person** in a caption, and never describe an individual's
   situation (never "this boy was using drugs"). Describe the *activity* instead:
   "Young people listening during the session."
3. **Photos of the work are better than posed photos** — people talking, writing,
   listening, working. They prove the work happened.
4. **Wide shots are safest.** A photo of the whole room shows turnout without
   singling anyone out.
5. Phone photos are fine. Hold the phone **sideways (landscape)** for the main photo.

## Questions people ask

**Will I break the website?**
No. You can only edit stories. Everything else is locked. If a story looks wrong, open
it again, fix it, and save — or delete it.

**Can I write it now and publish later?**
Write it, save it, and simply set the date. You can edit and re-save any time.

**Can I fix a spelling mistake after publishing?**
Yes. Open the story, correct it, click Save. The website updates within a minute.

**Can I delete a story?**
Yes — open it and use the delete option in the menu (three dots, top right).

**The photo is sideways or huge.**
Very large photos from a phone still work, but if a photo looks rotated, open it on your
phone, rotate and re-save it, then upload again.

**Something looks broken.**
Message Godwin with the name of the story. Nothing you do is permanent — every change
can be undone.

## What happens behind the scenes (you can ignore this)

When you click Save, your story is stored as a file in the website's repository. The
website then rebuilds itself automatically and publishes the new page — usually within
a minute. Each story gets its own address, for example:

```
ongeraubeho.rw/news/mentor-training-begins
```

That address is what you paste into Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram or LinkedIn. The
photo, headline and summary appear automatically when the link is shared.
